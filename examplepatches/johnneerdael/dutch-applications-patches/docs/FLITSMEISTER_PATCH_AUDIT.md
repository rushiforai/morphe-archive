### Installer attribution is a separate requirement

The initial missing launcher entry was an Android Auto host rejection before `BaseCarAppService` started. The inspected host classifies Flitsmeister as a templated navigation app; its unknown-sources fallback does not admit that category. This differs from media-app support such as YouTube Music. Maps and YouTube Music patch-source comparisons did not establish a reusable PairIP or external host-admission bypass.

On the tested Android 16 phone, setting only the installing package through ADB's `-i com.android.vending` was insufficient: the initiating installer remained Shell. The host reads the initiating installer first and, for the genuine Google Package Installer, checks its installing-package attribution. The successful, user-consented in-place update ran through the real Google system installer with a granted content URI. Its genuinely privileged Shell caller supplied `NOT_UNKNOWN_SOURCE=true`, avoiding the unknown-source permission precheck, and requested Google Play installer attribution. PackageManager then reported `com.google.android.packageinstaller` as initiator and `com.android.vending` as installing package. The app UID/data were preserved, and the host subsequently bound the real car service. This is a tested installation path, not a modification of Android Auto's trust checks or a guarantee for every installer/ROM.

A diagnostic Gadget failure was identified by its own native stack and excluded from attribution of the original driving report. Diagnostic libraries, phone captures, account data and signing keys are not part of the production patch bundle or repository.
